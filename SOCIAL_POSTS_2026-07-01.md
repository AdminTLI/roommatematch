## LinkedIn

Dutch headlines focus on how many student rooms get built each year. Less visible: municipal rules that decide whether three bedrooms can legally house three students.

Kences estimates a shortage of roughly 21,000 rooms now, with projections of 26,000 to 63,200 by 2032-2033 if private supply keeps shrinking (NOS, 2025). Behind those numbers sits a policy layer many renters never see:

- Permits required to rent to more than two unrelated tenants since the Wet betaalbare huur
- Parking norms that treat every extra roommate as a new car, even in bike-heavy student neighbourhoods
- A patchwork of local rules: Nijmegen easing sharing, Beverwijk tightening it in 2026

The LSVb surveyed fifteen student cities and found local co-living barriers still block supply that already exists inside existing buildings. In Utrecht, available student rooms fell an estimated 32% in 2025 (NOS).

Housing infrastructure is not only cranes and campus beds. It is also the administrative layer that decides whether a shared kitchen can legally feed three degree programmes.

## Instagram

You found a house with three bedrooms. Everyone is ready to sign.

Then someone asks: "Does the gemeente even allow three of us here?"

That question is more common than it should be. National policy talks about making room sharing easier. Local parking rules and permit requirements often say otherwise.

A few numbers worth knowing (source: Kences via NOS, 2025):
- ~21,000 student rooms short nationally right now
- In Utrecht, available rooms dropped an estimated 32% in one year
- Projections suggest the gap could widen sharply by 2032-2033

If you are house-hunting this summer: check the contract AND the household count. A fourth roommate without the right permit can create problems for everyone.
