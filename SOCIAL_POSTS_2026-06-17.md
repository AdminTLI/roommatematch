# Social posts — 2026-06-17

## LinkedIn

Roughly half of Dutch student rooms sit with private landlords. New ABF Research, reported by NOS on behalf of Kences and the housing ministry, shows more than 5,000 student homes sold in a single year — about 10,000 rooms — even as housing corporations keep building.

The geographic skew is stark: Amsterdam alone accounted for more than 2,000 private rental sales, with Rotterdam, Utrecht, and Groningen following. Kences warns the country could lose about 9% of student rooms within two years if the trend holds.

Policy is moving too. In April 2026, the housing minister announced faster prefab construction, easier house sharing (citing Utrecht's three-person model), and partial softening of the Wet Betaalbare Huur to keep rental stock on the market.

For anyone working on student wellbeing, internationalisation, or city talent pipelines: supply shrinkage in the private layer is not a side story. It shapes who can move out, what rent levels look like, and how quickly rooms turn over after graduation.

You hear "just find a room in Amsterdam" like it is a personality test.

Meanwhile, reporting on Kences and ABF data says private landlords sold thousands of student homes last year — and Amsterdam was hit hardest.

Less supply. Higher average rents (NOS cited roughly €683/month in early 2025). More students staying put because searching feels pointless.

If you are house-hunting this summer: you are not failing. The market is structurally tight, and policy changes will take time to show up in listings.
