# Social posts — 2026-07-08

## LinkedIn

Finding a student room in the Netherlands is only half the story.

In early 2026, the Landelijke Studentenvakbond (LSVb) reported **263 help requests from international students** through its Housing Hotline — illegal rents, unsafe conditions, bedbug infestations left untreated, and online scams.

The pattern is structural, not anecdotal. National monitoring (Kences, via NOS) still cites roughly **21,000** missing rooms, with stressed projections up to **63,200** by 2032–2033. When supply tightens, bargaining power shifts to whoever holds the keys.

Three signals worth tracking:

1. **Rights on paper ≠ rights in practice** — LSVb notes that ~80% of students who pursue legal steps via huurteams win, yet many international tenants never reach that stage.
2. **Huurteam retreat** — municipal tenant-support teams in cities like Utrecht and Rotterdam are shrinking just as misconduct caseloads rise.
3. **Information gaps persist** — ISO surveys (via NOS) have repeatedly flagged that international students lack clear guidance on finance, healthcare, and local complaint routes before arrival.

Housing misconduct is a wellbeing and retention variable, not a side file for internationalisation policy.
