# Social posts — 2026-06-24

## LinkedIn

Dutch student housing shortages are often reported as a single number: roughly 21,000 rooms, according to Kences data relayed by NOS in September 2025.

A less visible variable sits inside the stock that already exists. **57% of graduates were still living in their student room one year after finishing**, largely because they could not move into regular rental housing.

That turnover bottleneck matters because:

- Total room stock across twenty student cities fell by an estimated 13,500 units to about 322,400
- Private-sector occupancy dropped by 17,800 students year-on-year despite 5,000 new rooms being built
- Kences projects the shortage could reach 26,000 to 63,200 rooms by 2032-2033 under stressed supply assumptions

NOS also reported that Eindhoven plans roughly 5,400 student homes over eight years after an estimated 500 students ended their studies early because they could not find housing.

The full editorial breakdown, with sources and charts, is on the Domu Match blog.

## Instagram

You finished your degree. You still live in your student room. You are not alone.

Dutch monitoring data suggests more than half of graduates stay in student housing a year after graduating, often because there is nowhere affordable to move to.

That is not a personal failure. It is a market where:

- Rooms are scarce and getting pricier (average rent around 683 euros in early 2025, per Kamernet figures reported by NOS)
- Private landlords are selling student properties
- Incoming students compete for keys that never turn over

If you are stuck between graduation and your next lease, how long did your search take? And what would have helped most: lower rent, faster turnover, or more starter housing?

Drop a comment. No judgment, just curious what you are seeing in your city.
