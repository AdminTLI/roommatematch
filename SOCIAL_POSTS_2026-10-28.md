## 1. LinkedIn

On 25 October 2026, Dutch clocks went back an hour. Student houses felt it before most campuses did.

Earlier darkness compresses cooking, studying, gaming, and guests into the same early evening. Living-room peak hour moves forward. Quiet hours collide with dark-start nights. Artificial light becomes a shared resource, not a private preference.

Chronobiology and youth sleep data point the same way: short Dutch winters shrink daylight, and shared walls amplify the mismatch. The fix is not another themed party. It is a 20-minute wintertijd reset—priority nights for the sofa, quiet hours confirmed in clock time, warmer hallway light, and a bike-safety check for darker rides home.

Institutions track study load. Retention still rises or falls where the calendar meets the kitchen table.
