## 1. LinkedIn Post

Dutch student houses do not only run on leases and chore rotas. They run on tyres, lights, and the quiet logistics of getting four people to campus, the supermarket, and a late lecture without turning the hallway into a bike depot.

CBS data show the bicycle accounts for 27% of trips in the Netherlands — and that daily cycling distance drops sharply from October into December. That seasonal hinge is when outdoor culture has to become indoor agreements: where wet bikes go, who owns spare lights, and which evening is the low-effort living-room hang when terraces stop feeling spontaneous.

Campus research adds a second layer. Cycling can raise international students’ sense of belonging without automatically closing the contact gap with Dutch peers. Buying a bike is not the same as feeling at home at dinner.

Thought leadership takeaway for student affairs and housing teams: mobility culture is part of the living system. Autumn mid-terms test whether houses ever built an indoor script — not only whether residents “get along.”
