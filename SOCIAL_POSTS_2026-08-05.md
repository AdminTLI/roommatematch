## LinkedIn

**Private landlords are selling student homes faster than the Netherlands is building new rooms.**

Fresh Kences data, reported by NOS, paints a supply shock underneath the familiar shortage headlines:

- Roughly 5,000 student homes sold in one year (~10,000 rooms), 1.5× the prior year
- Total room supply in 20 student cities fell by an estimated 13,500 to ~322,400
- Amsterdam alone saw 2,000+ private rental sales; Rotterdam, Utrecht, and Groningen followed

The drivers are structural: the Wet betaalbare huur, higher box 3 taxation, and municipal permit rules for homes with three or more tenants. LSVb and Kences both argue for permit-free woningdelen (with fire-safety checks) to keep existing stock in circulation.

Eindhoven is pushing back with a 5,400-home plan over eight years. But nationally, the question is whether private rental stock stays in the pool at all.

## Instagram

You are not imagining it. The room hunt feels harder because the market itself is shrinking.

NOS and Kences report that private landlords are selling student houses in record numbers. New rules on rent caps and sharing permits mean fewer classic Dutch student flats stay on the market.

What that means in practice:
- Fewer listings every intake cycle
- More competition for the rooms that remain
- International students hit first (no parental-home fallback)

Some cities are responding with big build plans. Others are debating whether three students should be able to share without a separate permit.

If you are searching right now: verify every contract, watch for scams, and know your tenant rights before you transfer a deposit.
