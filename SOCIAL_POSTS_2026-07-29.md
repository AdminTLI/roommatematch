## LinkedIn

**Graduate holdover is the hidden layer of the Dutch student housing crisis.**

Kences reports that 57% of graduates still live in their student room one year after finishing their degree. They are not squatting. They are stuck between a student contract and a regular rental market with no affordable exit.

That matters because every room a graduate holds is one fewer for a first-year, an international arrival, or a domestic student who has been living at home longer than planned.

Other figures from the latest Landelijke Monitor Studentenhuisvesting (via NOS):

- Official shortage: ~21,000 rooms across 20 student cities
- Only 44% of students live out; 49% say they would want to (down from 52% / 59% eight years ago)
- Private-sector student housing down by 17,800 placements year-on-year

The policy conversation often focuses on new builds. Equally important is **release rate**: how many student rooms actually change hands each academic year.

## Instagram

You found a room. You finished your degree. Now what?

For more than half of Dutch graduates, the answer one year later is still: the same student room.

Not because they want the "student life" forever. Because the regular rental market has nowhere affordable to go.

That blocked turnover has a ripple effect:

- Fewer rooms open for new students each September
- More people give up searching before they even start
- International students face tighter arrival windows

If you are searching right now: you are not failing. The numbers show a structural squeeze, not a personal one.
